7 Indonesian Dishes You Must Try Before You Die

📅 October 3, 2026⏱ 6 min read🏷 Food Guide
Seven dishes, one delicious map of Indonesia.

Indonesia is the world's largest archipelago — 17,000 islands, 300 ethnic groups, and a food culture to match. Narrowing it to seven dishes feels almost unfair, but if you only ever taste seven Indonesian foods, make it these. Each one tells a story about the country, and each one has a tested recipe waiting for you below. Work through them in any order — there are no wrong answers here, only delicious ones.

1. Beef Rendang — The World's Most Delicious Food

Voted #1 in CNN's World's 50 Best Foods, rendang is slow-cooked beef braised for hours in coconut milk and spices until dark, dry, and impossibly rich. It is the crown jewel of Minangkabau cuisine and the dish Indonesia is most famous for. Get the authentic Beef Rendang Padang recipe →

2. Nasi Goreng — Fried Rice, Perfected

Every culture has fried rice; Indonesia's version — smoky from a screaming-hot wok, sweet from kecap manis, crowned with a fried egg — is the one travelers dream about. It is also the most forgiving entry point for beginners. Get the Nasi Goreng Jawa recipe →

3. Sate Ayam Madura — Charcoal-Grilled Satay

Skewers of chicken marinated in sweet soy and grilled over coconut-shell charcoal, served with thick peanut sauce and rice cakes. The Madura island version sets the gold standard. Get the Sate Ayam Madura recipe →

4. Gado-Gado — The Peanut-Sauce Salad

Blanched vegetables, fried tofu, tempeh, and hard-boiled egg under a blanket of sweet-spicy peanut sauce with shrimp crackers for crunch. Proof that Indonesian vegetarian food is anything but boring. Get the Gado-Gado Betawi recipe →

5. Soto Ayam Lamongan — Golden Chicken Soup

Turmeric-gold chicken broth with shredded chicken, vermicelli, cabbage, and a squeeze of lime — topped with crispy fried shallots and koya (prawn-cracker powder). Indonesia's ultimate comfort soup. Get the Soto Ayam Lamongan recipe →

6. Bakso Malang — Loaded Meatball Soup

Springy beef meatballs in savory broth, loaded with noodles, tofu puffs, wontons, and fried wontons — Malang city's maximalist answer to soup. Customize each bowl with sambal and vinegar at the table. Get the Bakso Malang Komplit recipe →

7. Es Cendol — The Dessert That Drinks Like a Meal

Pandan-flavored green rice-flour jellies, coconut milk, shaved ice, and liquid palm sugar — sweet, creamy, chewy, and ice-cold. The perfect ending after six savory courses. Get the Es Cendol Dawet Ayu recipe →

Planning a food trip? This list works geographically too: rendang (Sumatra), soto and bakso (Java), sate (Madura), gado-gado (Jakarta/Betawi), es cendol (nationwide). Seven dishes, one delicious map of Indonesia.

How This List Was Chosen

Seven dishes cannot represent 300 ethnic groups, so we chose by three criteria: global reputation (dishes the world already loves), everyday importance (dishes Indonesians actually eat weekly), and range (covering soup, grill, salad, rice, and dessert). You will notice Java and Sumatra dominate — that reflects population and restaurant presence abroad, not a claim that other islands matter less. Consider this a starting line, not a finish.

Honorable Mentions

Just missing the cut: Pempek (Palembang's fish cakes in dark vinegar sauce), Gudeg (Yogyakarta's sweet jackfruit stew), Sop Buntut (oxtail soup), and Martabak Manis (the thick sweet pancake). Any of them could headline a "next 7" list — and all have recipes on this site.

What to Drink With Each Dish

Pairing matters. Rich rendang loves Es Tebu (sugarcane juice) or plain hot tea to cut the fat. Nasi goreng and sate pair with es teh manis (sweet iced tea). Gado-gado's peanut richness cools down with Es Timun Serut (shaved cucumber drink). Soto ayam is traditionally followed by something sweet — Es Cendol is the classic. And bakso? Just add more sambal and a cold drink; the soup does the rest.

Cooking Your Way Through the List

Here is a suggested order if you are cooking all seven at home across a month. Week 1: Nasi Goreng — easy, fast, confidence-building. Week 2: Soto Ayam — learn to build bumbu and broth. Week 3: Sate Ayam — master the grill and peanut sauce. Week 4: Rendang — the graduation exam, worth every hour. Fill the gaps with Gado-Gado, Bakso, and Es Cendol on weekends. By the end, you will cook Indonesian food better than most restaurants outside Indonesia.

Where to Find These Dishes Abroad

No flight to Jakarta on the calendar? Indonesian restaurants cluster in cities with large diaspora communities — Amsterdam, Sydney, Los Angeles, and Kuala Lumpur all have excellent options. Look for places run by Indonesian families (the menu will often be in Indonesian first, English second) and order the dishes above by name; if rendang, soto, and sate are all done well, you have found a keeper.
